# Creator Validation Plan

**Status:** not started; no creator interviews or pilot commitments are claimed.  
**Purpose:** validate whether FANOVA solves a costly, recurring creator problem before investing in tokenomics or broader infrastructure.

## Target first partner

Prefer one independent webtoon/comic author or original-fiction creator who:
- already receives reader/fan-created continuations, fan art, theories, or remix requests, or has a community likely to submit them;
- controls the rights needed to permit the pilot material;
- can define a narrow set of allowed and prohibited uses;
- is willing to review a small number of submissions during a time-boxed pilot.

Do not start with major studios requiring long procurement or legal cycles. Do not use third-party IP without documented permission.

## 30-day experiment

### Phase A — Problem interviews (target: 5–10 creators)
Ask about actual past behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm:
1. Have fans ever sent derivative stories, art, characters, or continuations? Ask for the most recent example.
2. Where did submissions arrive, and how were they tracked?
3. What permissions did the creator give, and how were those permissions recorded?
4. What made a submission hard to accept, reject, credit, or publish?
5. Has a fan contribution ever created a copyright, moderation, safety, or brand concern?
6. What happens to an approved contribution today? Can it become official canon or be reused commercially?
7. How much time did the last review/coordination take?
8. What existing tool or manual process do they use? Why is it insufficient?
9. Would a permanent, public approval record help, or would privacy and flexibility make it undesirable?
10. What would need to be true for the creator to run a pilot or pay for a workflow like this?

Record anonymized notes only with consent. Do not present interest as a commitment.

### Phase B — Pilot commitment
Ask one qualified creator to provide or approve original pilot material and define:
- creator identity and authority to grant the permissions;
- the exact story/version and permitted derivative format;
- whether fan submissions may be displayed publicly;
- prohibited uses, commercial-use boundaries, attribution, and takedown expectations;
- review window and maximum submission count;
- how the creator can withdraw from the pilot;
- whether any compensation is involved (none is assumed by this plan).

Use a written permission statement reviewed by the participant. The prototype's checkbox is not a legal license.

### Phase C — Observed pilot
Run one small batch with real participants only after the permission terms are agreed. Record:
- invitations and actual participants separately;
- number of submissions received, reviewed, approved, and rejected;
- creator time spent per submission;
- participant comprehension of the permissions;
- whether the creator would repeat the process and why;
- failures, confusion, and privacy/legal concerns.

Report the denominator and collection method. Do not extrapolate a tiny pilot into market size.

## Suggested outreach note

> Hi — I'm building FANOVA, a small tool for creators who want to invite fan continuations under clear rules, review submissions, and keep attribution linked to the original story. I'm speaking with independent creators to understand how they handle fan works today. I'm not asking you to adopt a platform or promise a partnership. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about your current process? If there's a fit, we could later discuss a small pilot using only material you control and explicitly permit.

## Success criteria for the first validation cycle

These are targets, not existing results:
- 5–10 completed creator conversations;
- at least 3 describe a recent concrete problem with evidence of current workarounds;
- one creator agrees in writing to a tightly scoped pilot with original/authorized material;
- the prototype's rules and review flow are tested with at least one creator and one fan;
- the team can state one reason creators would use FANOVA instead of their existing process.

If the interviews do not support the problem, narrow or change the product hypothesis before building more infrastructure.
